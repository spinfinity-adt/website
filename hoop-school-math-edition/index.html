Hoop School: Math Edition!

What is Hoop School?

I picked up a hula* hoop at the age of six, and that was the start of a complex relationship with the art form - a manifestation of embodied cognition. It created a space for me to connect with myself, and for my mind and body to come together to achieve an understanding and mastery of a trick; science and mathematics in motion and emotion.

Although Mathematics is considered to be a logical and rational subject, our connections with it are deeply emotional. The experience of math is quite often connected with the teacher who taught it; the right teacher can open your heart to it, but when math is taught in a disembodied manner, students tend to shut out.

In my engineering education, I fell out of love with math; it didn’t feel like my body, my emotions and my being had a place. Through my master’s thesis, I have co-created a hoop-math pedagogy that challenges the mind-body duality predisposing traditional STEM education. This was built in the Just Futures Co-Lab at Srishti Manipal with a group of female students and volunteers from U&I, an NGO whose aim is to provide holistic education to underprivileged children.

Six sessions of “Hoop School: Math Edition!” took place over two weeks. Each session had an underlying math concept that was understood in a generative, rather than formulaic manner. We explored math through hoop moves, drawing, journaling prompts, and collaborative games. With each person being an active participant instead of a passive consumer, we challenged the banking model of education2. We generated knowledge in an engaged pedagogy through our bodies, while integrating ways of knowing with habits of being. Concepts were understood kinesthetically, through diagramming and visual representations, and in discussion and movement circles; circles showed up in more ways than one in this pedagogy.

A large part of this project also involved thinking through the methodological and ethical implications of working with children, and how to protect their privacy and identities while still doing justice to the created knowledge, and recognizing the value of each individual voice.

  • The first edition of Hoop School focused on emotional regulation with students of an NGO Swapaksh Foundation. There were 13 students from 12-17, and together we got in touch with ourselves and our emotions through the hoop!
  • I worked with Scooled and TALC (The Alternative Learning Community) and built Hoop School for a group of Neurodiverse students.

HOOP-MATH SUPERPOWERS ACTIVATED

In Mathematics for Human Flourishing, Francis Su takes the reader through Math virtues which are built in the pursuit of mathematics when it is grounded in basic human needs. I found a resonance with these through hooping as well! I’ve also added some mathematical thinking modes that can be cultivated through hooping. These superpowers as I like to call them, are in this edition arising from my embodied cognition and lived experience. In further editions of hoop school, I would like to have activities that gauge these and record them more collectively.

EMBODIMATH STRATEGIES

The future of embodied design in mathematics teaching and learning had 6 strategies that really helped me design activities for the course. I took the liberty of calling them ‘embodimath’ strategies, as that’s a mnemonic that my brain coined.

ACTION-BASED EMBODIMENT: Embodied designs set students up for correct intuitive responses before presenting them with analytic procedures that validate yet enhance these intuitions.
PERCEPTION-BASED EMBODIMENT: These designs target a/b concepts, such as likelihood (favorable events/possible events), slope (rise/run), density (total object area/total area), and proportional equivalence in geometrical similitude (a:b = c:d).
ACTION-COGNITION TRANSDUCTION: Action and cognition enjoy reciprocity: Just as cognitive processes can induce motor behaviors for performing goal-directed actions, performing actions can induce cognitive states that perform reasoning, problem solving and learning.
GESTURE SCHEMATIZING - Learners’ gestures can reveal knowledge not in speech, emerging or transitional knowledge, and how learners schematize information.
COLLABORATIVE GESTURE: The presence of multiple learners fundamentally changes the nature of how mathematical ideas can be embodied, in a way that is not describable as the sum of each individual’s actions.
GROUNDING ABSTRACTION: Abstract mathematical symbols are treated as physical objects distributed in space - grounding of abstraction in perceptual-motor actions.
VOLUNTARY MATHEMIZATION: In mathematics in particular, well-designed games can provoke players to voluntarily mathematize the digital context in a process called theorycrafting. (Making them speak in mathematical terms without realizing it!)
